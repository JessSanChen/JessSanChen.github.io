Research

I work on AI safety and biosecurity — keeping powerful, dual-use systems from failing when the agents around them don't cooperate.

AI safety

Two angles. Red-teaming: Spectre is a multi-agent system that generates diversified attacks to find where an aligned model breaks — building the adversary is how you learn the failure modes. Theory: many alignment failures are mechanism-design failures, and reward hacking is just an agent gaming a signal. My preprint and coursework circle this — incentive-compatible selection in federated learning, auctions with aggregated signals, matching under noisy preferences, and fairness that survives gaming. The common question: what a learning or allocation system does when its participants are strategic, and how to design one that holds up.

Biosecurity

Grounded in wet-lab work — viral circuit tracing at Georgetown (Cre-dependent pseudorabies tracing in mouse brain) and neural-interface research in Jia Liu’s lab (spike sorting and connectivity reconstruction from chronic mesh probes). Same instinct on the bio side: dual-use capability, and how you govern it. I want to work at the AI–bio safety boundary.

Also

Empirical political economy, mostly China and Taiwan — hukou reform and cross-strait semiconductor brain drain. Different tools, same instinct: how institutional design shapes what agents actually do.
