The Cross-Strait Brain Drain — Taiwan's Academia-Industrial Complex Against Geopolitical Risk

Political economy · Gov 1982, Harvard — sponsored by the Harvard Asia Center · 2022-12

PDF

Jessica Chen. Gov 1982, Harvard. Sponsored by the Harvard Asia Center. December 2022.

Summary

Between 2014 and 2019, roughly 3,000 semiconductor workers — about 7% of Taiwan’s chip workforce — moved to China. Beijing’s industrial policy has capital but a human-capital bottleneck it can’t buy its way out of, so it recruits directly from Hsinchu Science Park. Taiwan responded in 2022 by criminalizing “economic espionage” and core-technology transfer under the National Security Act.

The standard explanations for these flows are wage incentives and industry loyalty. I argue geopolitical risk is the more significant explanatory variable, and that it accounts for the resilience of Taiwan’s academia-industrial complex better than compensation does.

Method

This was primary-source work, not a literature paper. I interviewed 20+ stakeholders — academics, policymakers, and TSMC engineers — and aggregated migration and employment data from Taiwan’s National Statistics Bureau, with funding from the Harvard Asia Center.
