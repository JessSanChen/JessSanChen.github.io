Countering the Winner's Curse in Aggregated-Signal Common-Value Auctions

Research · CS 237, Harvard · 2024-12

PDF

Jessica Chen, Dean Huang. CS 237, Harvard.

Summary

Common-value auction theory has mostly analyzed the maximum-signal setting of Bulow and Klemperer (2002), where the common value is the highest bidder signal. We study the aggregated-signal setting instead: the common value is the sum of bidders’ signals. That’s a better model of situations where every participant holds a genuinely additive piece of information.

In the symmetric (non-sequential) case we quantify the magnitude of the winner’s curse, find pure Nash equilibria in toy games, and propose an inclusive posted-price design that eliminates the curse outright.

In the asymmetric (sequential) case we show that in equilibrium no bidder ever bids more than twice their own private signal, even though naive benchmarks suggest substantially higher bids.
