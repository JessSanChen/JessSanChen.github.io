Claim Denials — The Health Insurance Market from a Differential Learning Perspective

Research · Harvard · 2025-04

PDF

Jessica Chen, Eli Kirtley, Kasie Leung, Jessica Li, TingTing Yan. April 2025.

Summary

Insurers hold information about denial rates that consumers only learn through personal claim experience. We extend adverse selection with dynamic Bayesian learning: consumers update their beliefs about denial probability as claims come back, and drop out when beliefs cross a threshold.

The analytical toy model shows insurers maximize long-term profit by partially denying — enough to profit, slowly enough that naive consumers take a long time to notice. Simulating heterogeneous risk levels gives a non-monotonic risk pool: high-risk consumers exit first, which temporarily improves the pool, before low-risk consumers follow and the pool degrades. Apparent market stability masks the instability underneath.
