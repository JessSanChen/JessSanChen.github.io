## Summary

Insurers hold information about denial rates that consumers only learn through
personal claim experience. We extend adverse selection with dynamic Bayesian
learning: consumers update their beliefs about denial probability as claims come
back, and drop out when beliefs cross a threshold.

The analytical toy model shows insurers maximize long-term profit by *partially*
denying — enough to profit, slowly enough that naive consumers take a long time
to notice. Simulating heterogeneous risk levels gives a **non-monotonic risk
pool**: high-risk consumers exit first, which temporarily improves the pool,
before low-risk consumers follow and the pool degrades. Apparent market stability
masks the instability underneath.
