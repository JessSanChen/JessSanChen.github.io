## Summary

China's 2014 hukou reform invited municipalities under five million residents to
lower settlement barriers for rural migrants. Whether that rebalanced population
away from megacities toward regional centres is still open.

Existing work infers treatment status from headline population thresholds and
looks at the two years after the reform. Both choices are problems: hukou rules
are actually implemented at county and district level, so thresholds
misclassify; and two years is too short for migration effects that unfold
gradually.

I coded reform intensity city by city by **manually parsing local regulations and
news reports**, and tracked outcomes through 2023. The panel covers Beijing,
Shanghai, eleven Zhejiang cities, and eighteen Sichuan cities, with annual
resident population and GDP per capita, assembled from provincial statistical
yearbooks scraped from individual bureau websites.

Using difference-in-differences, I find **no average population gain in treated
cities**. Sub-provincial hubs like Hangzhou and Chengdu keep expanding while many
prefecture-level cities keep losing residents — partial liberalization did not
redirect migration downward.
