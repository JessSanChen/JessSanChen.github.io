# Research

<p class="lede">I work on AI safety and biosecurity — keeping powerful, dual-use systems from failing when the agents around them don't cooperate.</p>

## AI safety

Two angles. **Red-teaming:** [Spectre](projects/spectre-red-teaming.html) is a multi-agent system that generates diversified attacks to find where an aligned model breaks — building the adversary is how you learn the failure modes. **Theory:** many alignment failures are mechanism-design failures, and reward hacking is just an agent gaming a signal. My [preprint](projects/uber-stable.html) and coursework circle this — [incentive-compatible selection](projects/primes-federated-learning.html) in federated learning, [auctions with aggregated signals](projects/winners-curse-auctions.html), [matching under noisy preferences](projects/compute-marketplace.html), and [fairness that survives gaming](projects/fairness-online-gaming.html). The common question: what a learning or allocation system does when its participants are strategic, and how to design one that holds up.

## Biosecurity

Grounded in wet-lab work — [viral circuit tracing at Georgetown](projects/sst-neuroanatomy.html) (Cre-dependent pseudorabies tracing in mouse brain) and [neural-interface research](projects/jia-liu-lab.html) in Jia Liu's lab (spike sorting and connectivity reconstruction from chronic mesh probes). Same instinct on the bio side: dual-use capability, and how you govern it. I want to work at the AI–bio safety boundary.

## Also

[Empirical political economy](projects.html), mostly China and Taiwan — [hukou reform](projects/hukou-reform.html) and [cross-strait semiconductor brain drain](projects/cross-strait-brain-drain.html). Different tools, same instinct: how institutional design shapes what agents actually do.
