Jessica Chen

I work on AI x biosecurity: building the tools and benchmarks that catch AI misuse in the life sciences.

I’m an AI x Biosecurity Fellow at the Cambridge Boston Alignment Initiative, where I lead ASTRAL, an open-source scanner for biological misuse. I own the project’s data governance and design partnerships. I’m also first author on a benchmark for AI misuse monitors in dual-use research domains, with co-authors at NIST and RAND. This month I founded and ran the Boston Biosecurity Hackathon, a life-sciences expert elicitation study and field-building workshop.

More broadly, I study how AI agents behave when their context changes. Policy Under Pressure (under review; it grew out of an Apart Research sprint) is a 13-model, 26,398-episode study of how an agent’s tools, its operator-assigned persona, and its peers’ reports shape whether it follows rules and reports honestly. Before that I worked on mechanism design: what learning and allocation systems do when the agents inside them are strategic.

I’ve been interested in biosecurity since I was a teenager, when I spent summers at Fort Detrick (culturing bacteria and running PCR to look for Wolbachia in local insects) and the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research (immunology work around malaria). After that I did more bench work: brain slicing, patch-clamp recordings, and viral circuit tracing at Georgetown School of Medicine. At Harvard I rebuilt the Jia Liu Lab’s electrophysiology analysis pipeline.

I’m also Lead Researcher at AGI House, where I write research briefs, host podcast interviews and paper reading groups, and started the inaugural Fellows program. In a previous life I worked in restructuring banking at Lazard and credit investing at KKR, where the worst day of a CEO’s career was our normal Tuesday. At Lazard I was usually the sole analyst on bankruptcies and distressed situations, working daily with management, consultants, and counsel. I studied at Harvard, where I earned an S.M./A.B. in Computer Science and an A.B. in Economics and was a teaching fellow for CS 51, CS 136, and CS 182.

Outside work: I was born in Taiwan (and sometimes write about the political economy of the region [1][2]). I also dance, make digital art, ride motorcycles, and free-dive.

Policy Under Pressure: Social Context and Agentic Rule-FollowingUnder review

J. Chen, R. Acharya · Grew out of an Apart Research sprint · 2026

I designed and built the harness for a 13-model, 26,398-episode study of how an agent's tool, operator-assigned persona, and peer reports change whether it follows rules and reports honestly on itself. Agents were more than 4× as likely to break a rule by writing code as by calling a named tool.
